# Phase 0.4 acceptance: resin quantity and CTB motion/PWM

**Status:** Implemented and ready for user testing. The user has not accepted Phase 0.4, and no Phase 0.4 release has been published. Phase 0.5 remains outside this phase. See the [Phase 0.4 screenshots](phase-4-screenshots.md) for the synthetic UI fixtures.

## Behavior to review

- A material/color profile can enter bottle quantity in mL, g, or kg. Weight converts to volume only with that profile's positive finite uncured-liquid density in g/mL. Legacy mL profiles retain their quantity and cost behavior. Print mass is available only when density is known.
- CTB timing overrides can also specify seven optional per-layer motion fields: first and second lift distance and speed, second retract distance, and first and second retract speed. Ranges are inclusive, one-based model layers and exclude the startup dummy. Blank fields inherit independently from bottom/normal settings; later matching rules win per field.
- The PWM follow-up restores separate bottom and normal PWM controls for Simple CTB, matching the existing Two Stage and All Fields behavior. New bottom/normal entries use 1–100%; each timing range can optionally set `pwmPercent` from 0 to 100. Blank leaves an earlier matching PWM in place or inherits the bottom/normal default. Later matching ranges win independently of timing and motion fields. An explicit range 0% exports byte 0, while a legacy default of 0 keeps its existing full-power fallback. The enabled per-layer plan rounds percentage to a 0–255 byte, which the file-backed inspector displays with its percentage; legacy native defaults outside that plan retain their existing encoding. The startup dummy always exports PWM byte 1.
- Simple mode exposes lift distance and lift/retract speeds; retract distance equals total lift. Two Stage mode exposes both lift stages and the second-stage retract split. Nonpositive speed on a stage with travel, a retract split greater than total lift, and retained Two Stage overrides in Simple mode must be corrected before export.
- Printer profiles declare per-layer motion capability using a firmware string and explicit user confirmation. Unknown or unconfirmed firmware leaves range motion unavailable while bottom/normal motion and Phase 0.3 timing stay usable. This declaration is not verification of physical printer behavior.
- Range PWM follows the existing enabled timing support gate for CTB V4/V5, plain and encrypted. It does not require a per-layer motion firmware declaration.
- The raw LOD calculator uses effective motion after overlapping ranges. Applying to a default changes only that default. A mixed-motion range must be split into homogeneous groups before one calculated value can be applied. Calculation remains explicit; motion edits do not overwrite raw LOD or wait fields.
- The exported CTB and encoded-layer inspector use resolved motion. Estimates use the exported plan. Profile duplication, color variants, storage reload, and material/printer bundle import/export retain independent settings.

## Acceptance checks

1. Enter representative mL, g, and kg bottles with a known positive density; compare equivalent volume, print mass, and cost against hand calculations. Remove or invalidate density and verify that mL stays usable while weight has no assumed conversion. Reload and round-trip a legacy volume profile unchanged.
2. On CTB V4 and V5, including encrypted variants, exercise Simple and Two Stage, individual and inclusive range overrides, blank fields, overlapping ranges, the bottom/normal boundary, and startup dummy. Compare first, last-bottom, transition, and last model layers with resolved values.
3. Enter zero/negative speed for a moving stage, an impossible Two Stage retract split, and switch a Two Stage override to Simple. Verify visible errors and actionable clearing controls, and verify that export rejects invalid motion without silently clamping it.
4. Leave firmware unknown, then enter firmware without confirmation, then confirm it. Check the range controls and export gate in each state; repeat with an official printer profile and custom copy. Check bottom/normal motion and timing in restricted states. Treat firmware confirmation as user-declared support only.
5. Compare LOD calculation with effective overlapping motion and correction/rest inputs. Confirm a mixed range cannot receive one LOD, split it into homogeneous ranges, and apply separately. Edit motion afterward and confirm a stale/reapply notice while raw LOD and waits remain unchanged.
6. Save/reload, duplicate, create a color variant, and round-trip printer/material bundles. Independently decode exported CTB records and compare motion, timing, dummy indexing, inspector values, and time estimates. Record file-content results separately from hardware observations.
7. Where supported hardware is available, record printer model, firmware, CTB version, and observed per-layer motion behavior separately for each tested combination. Do not generalize from CTB encoding alone.
8. For the PWM follow-up, check separate Simple CTB bottom/normal PWM controls, storage reload and profile/bundle copy, blank inheritance, individual and overlapping inclusive ranges, and independent precedence against timing/motion fields. Check the timing gate across plain/encrypted V4/V5 and that range PWM remains available when motion firmware is unconfirmed. Reject nonfinite or out-of-range PWM without silently clamping.
9. Decode rebuilt plain/encrypted V4/V5 files independently, with and without dummy. Compare expected rounded 0–255 bytes for bottom, normal, a nonzero range, overlapping ranges, and explicit range 0%; confirm dummy byte 1 and the inspector's byte/percentage display. Separately check that a legacy default of 0 still exports full power. Record file-content results separately from hardware observations.

## Verification performed

The older results below predate the PWM follow-up. The rebuilt synthetic and app-produced file checks are recorded separately. The legacy default-0 path in an app export, the broader profile copy matrix, and hardware behavior remain pending. Earlier PWM comparisons did not verify explicit range 0%, Simple CTB controls, or the new range precedence.

- For the PWM follow-up, eight focused native tests and the explicit 16-fixture native export test passed. The rebuilt 16 CTB files were then independently decoded with UVtools CLI: all 72 file-layer PWM bytes matched the frontend plans. Plain and encrypted V4/V5, Simple and Two Stage, and dummy on/off produced model bytes 204, 128, 0, 230; dummy files began with byte 1. This verifies synthetic file content, including explicit range 0%, but does not establish UI behavior or physical printer support.
- The full TypeScript check, optimized frontend and native Windows builds, 37 focused frontend tests (34 core and 3 schema), and focused UI lint passed for the PWM follow-up.
- In the native Windows UI, a synthetic Simple CTB V5 encrypted profile showed separate normal 60% and bottom 80% PWM defaults. Its model-layer ranges 2–3 at 50% and 3 at 0% were visible with no motion firmware confirmation. Entering 125% showed an error; clearing the field restored Inherit, and resetting 50% saved. The [screenshots](phase-4-screenshots.md) capture these controls. The app produced a 41-layer encrypted CTB file; independent UVtools decoding matched every PWM byte: dummy 1, model layer 1 at 204, layer 2 at 128, layer 3 at 0, and layers 4–40 at 153. The encoded inspector displayed dummy byte 1 (0.4%), model layer 2 byte 128 (50.2%), and model layer 3 byte 0 (0.0%). File encoding and the UI do not establish physical printer support.

- The final Windows optimized frontend and native build passed without creating an installer bundle. TypeScript and 29 focused frontend tests passed. Focused lint passed; a baseline-relative audit of five changed UI files found no newly introduced diagnostics. Six focused native tests and 16 generated CTB fixture round trips passed. These are build and automated checks, not printer-hardware tests.
- Sixteen generated CTB files were independently decoded with the installed UVtools CLI. Fifteen numeric fields per layer were compared across 72 file layers: Z, exposure, raw LOD, three waits, seven motion fields, PWM, and derived first-stage retract distance. All 1,080 comparisons passed. Native tests assert the per-layer flag. UVtools `IsUsingGlobalParameters` reports value equality to globals; it is not a direct reading of that flag.
- In the native Windows UI, a synthetic printer bundle was imported and retained its fields after an app restart. A 1 kg bottle at 1.25 g/mL displayed 800 mL equivalent; clearing density showed that density was needed, and restoring it restored the conversion. Overlapping motion ranges 7–8 and 8 displayed their controls. The calculator blocked Apply for mixed motion, split the selection into 7–7 and 8–8, then applied 35.25 s raw LOD to layer 7.
- A 20 × 20 × 2 mm STL produced 0.8 mL, 1 g, and $0.04 in Prepare, Export, and Printing. A native CTB V5 encrypted export completed. Its encoded-layer inspector displayed layer 7 raw LOD 35.25 s, lift stages 2/5 mm at 120/240 mm/min, retract stages 6/1 mm at 180/60 mm/min, and waits 3/0.4/0.8 s. Independently decoding this app-produced file with UVtools passed 440 additional comparisons: 11 fields across 40 layers, covering bottom/normal values and the layer 7/8 overlap. Layer 7 LOD was 35.25 s while layer 8 remained 48.4 s, confirming that splitting and applying layer 7 preserved the other range.
- The full interactive invalid-input, capability, and smaller-window matrix remains unperformed, although automated validation covers invalid motion and capability gating. The broader profile duplication/color/bundle matrix and physical printer behavior remain pending. The synthetic printer fixture does not establish support for any real firmware. No hardware capability has been verified by the app.
